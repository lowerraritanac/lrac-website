## Week Recap

115 miles total this week, 96 in the 5 days prior, and 700 in the last 6 weeks.

While I’ve been slacking on documenting via writing logs this month, I have not been on the
roads. More Double days, 11 miles at a 4:59 average pace on Saturday, and a deep feeling that
something special is within.

Special for many reasons, but here are the important ones:

**1. I have won already.**

Meaning, I could go out there and run 2:35 but still know that I have what it takes to be great.
That realization from this block is remarkable and far beyond my expectations from a passion as
mundane as running.
**2. The overnight success**

I believe deeply in my ability to turn some heads. While a time nearing 2:12 isn’t professional
caliber anymore, it’s getting there. And perhaps the 2500 miles in the last 25 weeks, 12,000 in
the last 3 years, and so on… will be a shock when a 22-year-old 14-minute 5k guy does it. Or so
I hope.
**3. Limits are psychological**

If you try to run 3:59 in the mile, you may end up running 4:01. If you try to run 2:15, you may
run 2:16. So why not try to run 2:12, or 2:10 next, or 2:08… You get the gist. An Olympic Trials
qualifier was the dream, but it was never near the limit, and the same goes for the next one.
**4. The village**

This sport seems individual: I ran the miles. I wrote the training. I made the sacrifices.
Except, none of that is true.
I ran the miles by watching family, friends, and teammates do it first.
I wrote the training by trusting my 6 past coaches, dozens of training partners, and hundreds of
random takes from people I’ve never met.
I made the sacrifices, except it was friends, teammates, and family allowing me to do so, making
most of the goal selfish and never a sacrifice.

Intentional or not, every action by those around you makes a performance, not the one achieving
it. That’s beautiful, and odds are if you’ve read this week's training log, you’ve made it happen.

The closing remark on this penultimate log can be:
I’d rather fly and die than never fly at all.
Here’s to attempting to fail.
