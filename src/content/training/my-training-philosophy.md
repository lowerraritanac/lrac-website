Bread consists of flour, water, and salt. A baker may toss in sugar, spices, or some magical touches to produce the finished product, yet the core will always be unreasonably simple.

Imagine all the bread you’ve consumed: maybe homemade, perhaps freshly baked from a bakery, or even Wonder Bread, or Subway, or many other shitty loaves of bread.

I’ve long thought the result was due to a recipe, a temperature, or an extravagant process. Today I believe intent is the secret ingredient. A craft baker refines their process to make a perfect loaf; a billion-dollar conglomerate manufactures at scale to create bioengineered bread; and a private equity owner aims to slash costs to produce perfectly chemical-smelling sugar bread (except Ireland, where legally we must call this cake).

What am I rambling on about? Oh, the intent of it all. Each of these parties knows what they seek to produce and the costs of it, so they focus on that task. That is, if the baker is successful.

The marathon, for me, is sourdough bread: flour, water, salt. No bullshit. One must meticulously monitor the starter, measure the flour and water, and salt to taste. Nevertheless, it is quite simple.

Still, many who possess all the flour, water, and salt in the world completely fuck it up. They fail to launch, overcomplicate the spices of it all, or just burn the loaf.

All of this to say, I’m just baking some bread, and whatever I get, I’ll eat.

**Flour:** the miles run, the volume, the boring work, the early mornings, the late nights.

Just get out the door and run, and run a lot.

**Water:** the recovery, sleeping plenty, eating well, being mentally stable, and emotionally strong.

**Salt:** the proper workouts, big long runs, nutritional timing, heart rate and lactate tracking, and whatever fads are deemed relevant in due time.

Then you must actually cook the damn thing. Race, execute, and don’t overcook what commanded all this great labour. After all, it’s sourdough, not sugar slop Subway that’s ready from a microwave with plastic American cheese and mystery meat.

Finally, you eat the bread. If the recipe is executed with intent, then bask in the satisfaction of a job well done. If the result is off, toss some butter on it and try again next time.
