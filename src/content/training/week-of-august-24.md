120 miles run. Mon. 21, Tues. 10, Wed. 21, Thurs. 12, Fri. 18, Sat. 24, Sun. 14

Perhaps this endeavour isn’t easy. It is, however, simple.

The body is willing to give you a lot; the tricky part is deciphering how much, how hard, and when. There are moments where the body or mind is willing to give you more, so you must take advantage, while always leaving a surplus to spare.

For example, on Monday I had the opportunity to explore the most beautifully remote sections of the Arctic, so why not seize that day? However, Tuesday, my body and soul felt like it had been crushed by a semi-truck. So what is the adequate response to that? Just move, just run, and just keep moving. Reflecting on just one week of life and training is humorous because the highs and lows contrast so much, yet they are so close together. Yet, the weekly homeostasis remains quite even, which maybe is why I love the pursuit so dearly.

Last week I dreamed of Walter Mitty, or even Anthony Bourdain, dreaming of being a person to seize life on a whim. I still do, but I feel as if the story needs to build more. I need to get punched in the gut a few more times, fail a few more times, win a few more times, learn a few more times.

Now I dream of the running greats. How glorious a victory would feel against someone whose sole purpose is to compete. Dreaming of a corporate worker upsetting a titan. Dreaming of a nobody making the trials. Dreaming of a zero time state champ, zero time national qualifier, never a number one guy, out for blood at the Olympic Trials.

To me, earning a spot in the arena means everything. Qualifying for the trials means a spot on the start line. A spot on the start line is a lottery ticket. One certainly can’t win the lottery without first buying a ticket. So each mile, each workout, each week, each race, and so on, is a minuscule step towards the summit.

Onwards.
